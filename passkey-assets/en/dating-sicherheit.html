Safe dating

Thestia connects people who do not know each other yet. That is exactly where the opportunity lies – and the risk. This page summarises what matters before the first chat and the first meeting. It is the detailed version of the guidance in the app.

In short

Nobody has to pressure you into anything – neither into a meeting nor into pictures nor into money. Your data belongs to you. You may leave, report and block at any time, without having to justify yourself.

Before you get started

Online dating brings people together who would otherwise never meet. But it also brings people who want to exploit exactly that. That is no reason to be mistrustful of everyone – but it is a reason to pay attention to a few things.

  • Time is your ally. Anyone who puts pressure on you, becomes very personal very quickly or pushes you into a meeting is usually after something other than getting to know each other.
  • A profile is no proof. Photos, names and professions can be faked. Trust grows through repeated, consistent conversations – not through a nice picture.
  • You set the pace. It is perfectly fine not to answer questions, to postpone a meeting or to end a conversation.

Not everything is real

Fake profiles are common. Some want attention, some want money, some collect pictures or personal data. Typical warning signs:

  • The profile seems too perfect, the photos look like they come from a catalogue, and there is hardly any information about the person.
  • The person dodges the issue when it comes to video calls, mutual acquaintances or concrete meetings.
  • After a few messages you get a declaration of love, a request for money or the suggestion to continue the chat somewhere else.
  • You are pressured into sending pictures, or you receive intimate pictures unasked.

Ask for a short video call or a meeting in a public place before you share personal things. If the person permanently avoids that, that is information – not a trivial matter.

Your data belongs to you

Phone number, address, employer, ID photos and location are sensitive. Share them only once you know the person well – and even then only if you really want to.

  • Screenshots happen faster than you think. Even “disappearing” messages or pictures can be copied. Do not send anything whose spread you could not live with.
  • Be careful with links and files. Unknown links can lead to fake pages or install malware.
  • Use secure messengers. Signal and Threema are better suited for exchanging things after you have got to know each other than platforms without end-to-end encryption.
  • Your number stays with you. In Thestia you can store phone numbers and handles locally on a voluntary basis. They are not sent to our servers and are only shared when you actively tap “Share” or “Copy”.

The first meeting

A first meeting should be plannable and manageable for you.

  • Meet during the daytime in a public place where other people are – café, park, museum.
  • Tell someone you trust where you are, who you are meeting and when you want to be back.
  • Arrange your way there and back yourself. You do not have to tell anyone your address – not even when you are being picked up.
  • Stick to your own drink and do not leave it unattended.
  • If you feel uncomfortable, you may leave. You owe nobody an explanation or another drink.

Boundaries and consent

A yes applies only to exactly what is happening right now. A no, a hesitation or a withdrawal is a no. This applies to messages, pictures, meetings and everything that comes afterwards.

  • Nobody owes you pictures, time or closeness – and you do not owe them either.
  • Pressure is not getting to know each other. Anyone who talks you into something you do not want does not respect you.
  • A “no” can come at any time – even in the middle of a date. You do not have to stay polite if you feel pressured.
  • If you shared something and regret it: you can block and report the person. Asking for help is not a weakness.

Scams and sextortion

In a romance scam, trust is built up over weeks, then there comes an emergency, an investment, an invoice or a purported package. In sextortion, intimate pictures or chats are used as leverage. In both cases the following applies:

  • Do not pay. A payment will normally not end the blackmail; it only confirms that it works.
  • Break off contact, block and report the profile in the app and on the platform through which the contact is being made.
  • Secure the evidence (screenshots, profile link, payment details) before you block.
  • Get help. You are not to blame. In case of threats, stalking or violence, the police (110) or the emergency services (112) will help.

Getting help

You can report and block profiles in Thestia at any time, without having to justify yourself. Reports are reviewed confidentially. If you are in acute fear or are being threatened, do not wait for a reply in the app:

  • Police: 110
  • Emergency call / ambulance service: 112
  • Telefonseelsorge: 0800 111 0 111 or 0800 222 2 222 (free, around the clock)
  • Hilfetelefon Gewalt gegen Frauen: 116 016 (free, around the clock)
  • Nummer gegen Kummer for young people: 116 111

In the child safety section you will find the reporting route for reports of child abuse as well. In the app there are further immediate measures under Profile → Safety Center.
